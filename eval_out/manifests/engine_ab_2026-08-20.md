# Deterministic-engine A/B (PP-OCRv6 / PP-OCRv5 / RapidOCR) — 2026-08-20

**Runner:** `eval_out/engine_ab.py` (per [rapidocr_eval_plan.md](../../Docs/dev_notes/rapidocr_eval_plan.md);
per-item rows `eval_out/engine_ab/results.csv`). **Machine:** Luke's laptop. **Method:**

## Scope correction found pre-run (the campaign's 7th "audit the defaults" catch)

paddleocr has been pinned at **3.7.0 since 2026-06-28** (constraints.txt) and 3.7.0's
default en models are **PP-OCRv6_medium_det/rec** (confirmed live + by both machines'
`~/.paddlex` caches). So every July benchmark labelled "PaddleOCR" — stream A's FUNSD
0.810 / SROIE 0.901, the stream-E archive, the paddle_skip pricing — was **already
PP-OCRv6_medium**. The plan's "null hypothesis: add a v6 arm" was satisfied by accident;
the docs' belief that the baseline was v5-era was stale. Consequently the informative arms
here are: the current default (v6m), a **v5_server arm via the new `det_model`/`rec_model`
config knob** (recovering the generation delta), and **RapidOCR 3.9.2** (whose bundled
ONNX rec model is **PP-OCRv6_small** — the port is a generation-current, smaller tier).

## Verdict against the pre-registered criteria

- **RapidOCR default (small rec): NOT adoptable as-is.** Faster: emphatically (×16.2
  FUNSD / ×21.7 SROIE — 0.6s vs 11–12s/page, CPU EP, no CoreML claimed). Quality-equal:
  **no** — recall −0.021 / −0.027 vs the default, outside the plan's ~0.01 bar. Confound:
  small rec tier vs paddle's medium — resolved the same day, below.
- **rapid_medrec — the tier-confound resolver (same-day follow-up): ALL THREE
  pre-registered criteria MET.** Same engine, rec model = the RapidAI zoo's SHA256-pinned
  **PP-OCRv6_rec_medium** ONNX (det stays small — the word-count diagnostic showed rapid's
  detector already finds 98.8% of paddle's word volume, so the gap was recognition-side).
  Result: recall **−0.0087 FUNSD (within the ~0.01 bar) / +0.0047 SROIE (beats paddle,
  precision too)** at **×4.5 / ×7.1 faster** (2.41s / 1.72s vs 10.95s / 12.27s). Geometry:
  pre-verified. **Tail check (the paddle_skip lesson):** per-item Δrecall vs paddle_v6m has
  **median 0.0000**, worst −0.090, best +0.091, 9/60 items below −0.05 broadly matched by
  gains on the other side — symmetric model-disagreement noise, not a one-sided failure
  mode. **So the adoption case for det/rec on Latin-script routes now exists.** Recommended
  pre-adoption confirmation (cheap at 2s/page): the full-set run (FUNSD 199 + SROIE 973)
  on the same runner, plus fold the engine choice into a proper config knob (today's
  `set_rec_tier` is an eval-side switch). Adoption itself — flipping `prefer_rapidocr` for
  Latin routes — is a default change and lands as its own PR (Luke).
- **The generation delta is the real headline:** v5_server trails v6_medium by **13–37
  recall points** (SROIE collapses to 0.52). Staying current on Paddle generations matters
  far more than the engine question — retro-vindicating the accidental v6 default, and
  principles.md's "audit the defaults, periodically".
- **Env-pinned limitation (recorded, not skipped silently):** the plan's PP-OCRv6
  tiny/small Paddle tiers (the 6.1× M4 vendor claim) are not in paddleocr 3.7.0's
  registry; testing them needs a paddleocr upgrade — a separate, lock-changing decision.
  The rapid arm *is* a v6-small datapoint by proxy (ONNX runtime).
- **Verification #1 gap (documented in engines/rapid.py):** the rapidocr pip package
  bundles en/ch models only — per-script recognisers (Thai/Cyrillic/Arabic) would need
  sourced model files, so non-Latin routes stay on PaddleOCR regardless.
- Layout/table were **not** part of this A/B (per plan: reading-order head portability
  unverified — layout stays PaddleOCR).

−0.0001, which is exactly why the criterion is per corpus.

### Was the seeded n=30 representative?

- funsd: n=30 mean Δrecall −0.0078 vs the other 169 items −0.0130 (full −0.0122)

The seeded sample was mildly favourable to rapid_medrec on forms — not wrong, but the bar
(~0.01) sits right where n=30 sampling error lives; the pre-registered full-set confirmation
was the right guard.

### Where FUNSD loses (the 25 items beyond −0.05)

About a third are **detection-side** (rapid's hyp word count ≥15% below paddle's on the page:
the small ONNX detector misses lines on some forms), the rest **recognition-side** (similar
word counts, fewer matched). Corpus-wide rapid finds 99.2% of paddle's word volume (28,587 vs
28,807) — the detector fails on a tail, not on average. That split motivated the
`rapid_medboth` arm below.

re-timed idle because no claim rests on it once the quality read fails.

### Verdict against the pre-registered criteria

- **(a) faster:** rapid_medrec yes — ×4.5 FUNSD / ×7.1 SROIE (laptop n=30, same machine).
  rapid_medboth: ~×2 at best (5.2 s under load vs paddle 10.95 s idle) — most of the small detector's gain is gone.
- **(b) recall within ~0.01 + reading order preserved:** rapid_medrec **NOT met on FUNSD**
  (−0.013, CI [−0.020, −0.005], one-sided tail); met on SROIE (+0.003); reading order
  preserved on the labelled set. rapid_medboth: NOT met on FUNSD (−0.011, tail 22 vs 7); SROIE not run (moot).
- **(c) geometry equivalent:** pre-verified for det boxes; the mixed-page supersede path was
  a wiring gap, now fixed (#48).

**→ RapidOCR is NOT adopted for det/rec on this evidence.** The det/rec swap is per
component, not per corpus, and forms are the document class PaddleOCR leads on (stream A).
PaddleOCR (PP-OCRv6_medium under the 3.7.0 pin) stays the deterministic engine. RapidOCR
remains the wired A/B option (`prefer_rapidocr`), and after #48 it actually works with the
VLM. What would reopen the question: an ONNX detector tier that keeps the small detector's
speed without its forms tail, or a deployment where receipts-class documents dominate and
1.3 recall points on forms is an acceptable price for ×5 on the CPU-bound stage — Luke's
call, on this record. No adoption PR. The eval-side tier switches (`set_rec_tier` /
`set_det_tier`) stay eval-side and un-fingerprinted, which is fine while nothing adopts them.
